The busy beaver

Of every Turing machine with four states and two symbols that halts when started on a blank tape, this one runs the longest: 107 steps, leaving 13 ones. No four-state machine does better; that was proved in 1983. Here the same small trained transformer runs it, one forward pass at a time, without ever having seen it.

Why this is a hard test

A busy beaver is the halting machine of a given size that runs longest from a blank tape (Radó, 1962). Whether a given machine ever halts cannot be decided in general, so these numbers are found one size at a time, by ruling out every other machine. Two states: 6 steps. Four: 107 steps, 13 ones (Brady, 1983). Five: 47,176,870 steps, settled only in 2024 by a collaborative, computer-checked proof.

The champion sweeps back and forth across a growing block of 1s, as the picture above shows, and then halts. The network never saw it: it trained on random machines, with tapes of up to six cells. With its prelude the champion has five states, and its tape reaches 14 cells.
